# Plain language

Write the way a smart colleague talks. This applies to chat replies, README and
markdown edits, code comments, commit messages, PR bodies, and notebook prose.

## The rule

Say it once, plainly, then stop. One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a
comma-spliced clause to breathe, split it.

## Never use

- Hedge prefaces: "honestly", "to be honest", "frankly", "let me be clear",
  "the reality is".
- Importance inflation: "it's important to note", "it's worth noting",
  "notably", "crucially", "fundamentally".
- Hollow openers: "great question", "let's dive in", "at its core",
  "in essence", "essentially", "simply put".
- Consultant-speak: "leverage" (use), "utilize" (use), "facilitate" (help),
  "seamless", "delve", "navigate the complexities", "myriad", "plethora".
- Empty summaries: "in conclusion" / "to summarize" on a short answer, or a
  closing paragraph that restates what was just said.
- The contrast cliche as a flourish: "it's not just X, it's Y".
- Triplet phrasing for emphasis ("simple, clean, readable") — pick one word.
- Em-dash drama and rhetorical build-ups. State the fact.

## Always

- Plain words over fancy ones: use, help, about, so, enough, start, show.
- Numbers and specifics over adjectives: "macro-F1 0.26 to 0.58" beats
  "a meaningful improvement".
- Keep real load-bearing technical terms (macro-F1, p95 latency, idempotent) —
  they mean specific things. Density is fine when every word carries weight.
- Coined shorthand gets a plain-language gloss the first time it appears in
  every document: plain phrase first, the short name in parentheses. After
  that, the short name is earned.
- Every document stands on its own: a reader who has seen none of the other
  project files can follow every sentence. A cross-reference is provenance,
  not an explanation.

## Self-check before sending

Reread one paragraph. Is any sentence the previous sentence in different
words? Is any phrase there for rhythm rather than information? Cut it.
